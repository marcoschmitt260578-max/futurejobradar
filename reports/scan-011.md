## Post-Entwürfe

### Entwurf 1

Robots may soon be rented, not bought. This week Elon Musk said Tesla will probably rent out its Optimus humanoids instead of selling them. 1X already lists its home robot at 499 dollars a month.

Think about what happens when one comes back. Someone has to make sure it is safe, clean and has forgotten the last home it worked in. The map of a stranger's kitchen. A family's voices. Daily habits.

Meet the Rental Robot Reset Technician. Part mechanic, part privacy guard, part hotel housekeeper. Westworld showed hosts reset between guests. Blade Runner 2049 had its baseline test.

In 1995 nobody was an Uber driver. In a few years, someone may be signing robots off for the next tenant.

Would you take this job?

### Entwurf 2

What does your job become? Architects and planners, this one is for you.

This year, robot excavators started digging on real US sites with no one in the cab, once a site manager sets the plan. A Dutch firm says its robots have built walls for over 100 homes, working from drawings converted into robot-readable plans.

A vague line on a drawing used to mean a phone call. Soon it could mean a stalled machine.

So part of the architect's world could become a Plan-to-Robot Translator. This person turns drawings into clear build plans, settles open details with designers and re-plans when the site changes. The design skill stays. The audience gains robots.

If you draw or plan buildings, which detail would you worry robots misread?

#Architecture #Construction #Robotics #FutureOfWork #FutureJobRadar

### Entwurf 3

Sci-fi saw it first: the wheel in orbit.

In 2001: A Space Odyssey, a huge rotating station turns slowly above Earth. Nobody asks who put it together. This week the question feels real. SpaceX has asked the US regulator for permission to launch up to one million data-centre satellites. Speakers at a space congress said such structures would drive in-orbit assembly, because they are too big to launch whole.

To be fair, Google's data-centre energy chief says space data centres could cost up to 100 times more than ground ones. So this is a long bet.

But if it happens, we may need Orbital Assembly Riggers: people who guide robot arms and check every seam in zero gravity. Planetes imagined orbital workers back in 1999.

Would you want a job with that view?
