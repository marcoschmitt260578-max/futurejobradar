## Post-Entwürfe

### Entwurf 1

Job of the Week: Practice-Home Set Dresser.

On Tesla's Q3 call, Elon Musk said the company may need roughly 10,000 to 20,000 Optimus robots practising tasks through self-play, in what he calls an Optimus Academy. Meanwhile, people filming chores at home to train humanoids say it is hard to come up with enough variety in a small flat.

So here is the second-order job: someone who designs the practice houses. Think film set dresser, but the audience is a robot. A wobbly chair. A sticky drawer. Toys on the stairs. A dishwasher that never closes the same way twice.

The best robots will not learn from perfect rooms. They will learn from believable mess, and mess takes craft.

Would you take this job?

### Entwurf 2

What does your job become? Farmer.

This month, self-driving tractors and AI weeders were on show in Yakima. One report noted these tractors can work three shifts a day. A farmworker nonprofit at the same event said it wants worker expertise built into the technology.

Here is one possible future: the Farm Craft Archivist. Someone who sits with a veteran picker, films how a thumb tests a berry, and turns that craft into skills a robot can learn. The worker is credited and paid for the knowledge.

The tractor drives itself. The judgement still comes from people who have spent decades in the field. The farmer's knowledge becomes the most valuable data on the farm.

What would you teach a robot after thirty years in your job?

#Farming #AgTech #FutureOfWork #FutureJobRadar

### Entwurf 3

Sci-fi saw it first: drug glands.

In Iain M. Banks' Culture novels, people can release custom drugs from glands inside their own bodies. Medicine is personal and immediate.

We are not there. But in 2025 a baby called KJ received a gene-editing therapy designed only for him, built in about six months by dozens of scientists. The FDA has since published draft guidance for approving therapies like it.

If many custom cures become routine, someone has to run each one like a production: the lab, the manufacturer, the hospital, the funding. I call that job the Bespoke Cure Producer.

Fiction gave us the dream. Now we need people to build the workflow.
