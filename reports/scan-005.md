## Post-Entwürfe

### Entwurf 1

Job of the Week: Orbital Dark-Sky Mediator.

SpaceX has filed plans for up to a million orbital data-centre satellites. Google just launched its first TPU into orbit. And astronomers have modelled what sunlit satellite rings could do to the twilight sky.

So a new question appears: who agrees on how bright the sky is allowed to be?

The mediator sits between satellite operators, observatories and regulators. She runs sky-brightness simulations, negotiates observing windows and writes the rules both sides can live with.

Liu Cixin's Three-Body Problem imagined a sky that becomes a contested stage. We may get a gentler version of that story, with a negotiator at the table.

Would you take this job?

### Entwurf 2

What does your job become? Nurse or carer.

More older people now live with a home robot that talks, reminds and keeps them company. One small study followed this for 18 months and saw sustained engagement. And one in three Americans aged 50 to 80 say they feel lonely.

Here is the question nobody asks yet: what happens when the robot gets an update, or is replaced?

That could become a care job of its own. A Companion Robot Handover Specialist helps the person and the family decide what the new robot remembers and what it lets go. It sits next to nursing, not instead of it.

Care skills may be the ones that grow, not shrink.

#Care #FutureOfWork #Robotics #FutureJobRadar

### Entwurf 3

Sci-fi saw it first: Dune and the data centre.

In Dune, the Fremen reclaim every drop of water. Nothing is wasted. Now look at our AI buildings. Almost all the electricity a data centre uses ends up as heat.

Germany now requires new data centres to reuse a rising share of that heat, and a Finnish project aims to warm about 250,000 people with it.

Someone has to match the heat to the neighbours: homes, greenhouses, pools, factories. We could call that person a Data Heat Matchmaker.

A job that did not exist in 1995, and that a desert planet would have understood.
