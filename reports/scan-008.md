## Post-Entwürfe

### Entwurf 1

Job of the Week: Memory Cue Designer.

Picture a woman in early Alzheimer's who lives at home. A robot helps her find her glasses and reminds her to drink water. But who decides which words it uses, which photo it shows, which song it plays when she feels lost at 4 p.m.?

That is the Memory Cue Designer. The signals are real: the US National Institute on Aging is funding work to teach the Stretch robot to support people with mild cognitive impairment, and AI callers now phone seniors every day.

Sci-fi saw it early. In Severance, people live on carefully managed scraps of who they are. In Robot & Frank, an old man and a robot build a daily routine.

The designer works with family and nurses, builds cues from the person's own life story, and tests them gently. Warm, human, a little technical.

Would you take this job?

### Entwurf 2

What does your job become? Excavator operator.

On some US sites the excavator cab is already empty. Retrofit kits let machines dig on their own, and one maker says the goal is for one operator to run several machines. The cab disappears, but the work does not.

I think the operator becomes a site conductor: sets the plan, watches a fleet from a tablet, steps in when the ground surprises it. And a new neighbour appears, the Spoil Matchmaker, who finds a home for the million cubic yards of earth these machines move, before the trucks even start.

The skill that stays is reading the ground. Machines are fast. People know what a site feels like.

What would you add to the list?

### Entwurf 3

Sci-fi saw it first: who controls the robot?

In The Murderbot Diaries, a security robot carries a governor module that limits what it may do, and the story starts when it hacks that module. In July 2026 the US FCC decided that new foreign-made humanoids and quadrupeds cannot get approval for sale, citing hacking and supply chain risk. Robots already approved stay legal.

Now every buyer of a humanoid will ask: where was it made, what is inside, who can update it? That creates a job I call the Robot Provenance Auditor. Part customs officer, part software detective, part storyteller who explains a robot's family tree to a hospital or a factory.

Fiction asked the question first. The job comes second.

Which robot would you want papers for?
