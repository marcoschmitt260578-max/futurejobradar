## Post-Entwürfe

### Entwurf 1

Job of the Week: Master Trade Recorder.

One US report says the country needs 1.7 million new skilled trade workers every year through 2035, while training produces just over half of that. Another points to aging journeymen and the data-center building boom.

Meanwhile, robots already lay out floor plans on slabs at big contractors. A robot only learns what someone shows it.

So picture a person who follows master tradespeople with cameras and sensors, asks why they did it that way, and turns decades of know-how into lessons for robots and apprentices. Part film maker, part teacher, part site foreman.

Ferrix, the salvage town in Andor, keeps its craft in people's hands. We may need to write that craft down before it walks off site.

Would you take this job?

### Entwurf 2

What does your job become? Truck driver.

This week Kodiak and IKEA said they plan fully driverless freight in Texas later this year, with nobody in the cab on a 219-mile stretch of Interstate 45. In Ohio, Einride has two cabless electric trucks hauling between warehouses every day.

Here is what I keep wondering. A driver walks around the truck before every trip: tyres, coupling, sensors, load. When the cab is empty, who does that walk?

My idea: an Empty-Cab Inspector at the hub, who clears each truck to roll and keeps the record that software and insurers trust. Drivers know trucks better than anyone, so they could be first in line.

That is my idea, not a forecast. What would you add to the inspector's checklist?

#FutureOfWork #Logistics #Autonomy #Trucking #FutureJobRadar

### Entwurf 3

Sci-fi saw it first: Murderbot.

In Martha Wells' story, a security unit spends its days watching camera feeds and stepping in before its clients get hurt. It would rather watch soap operas, but it keeps showing up.

Now look at home care. A Seoul pilot is trying radar sensors that spot pre-fall movement. A 2026 study of 48 older adults used home sensors, and the alerts led nurses and community health workers to make 72 interventions.

Sensors can see trouble coming. Someone still has to knock on the door, with a calm voice and a cup of tea.

I call that job the Pre-Fall Responder. It is care work, made faster and kinder by machines.

Who do you know that would be great at it?
