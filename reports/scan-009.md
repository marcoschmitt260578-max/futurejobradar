## Post-Entwürfe

### Entwurf 1

New South Wales has become the first Australian state to legalise human composting, which turns remains into soil. In Maryland, one facility is designed to hold up to 126 vessels at a time.

Here is the question I cannot stop asking: what happens to the soil afterwards?

Meet the Soil Memorial Steward. This person helps a family decide where the soil goes: a memorial grove, a garden, a shared forest. They check the soil, handle the permits and keep the living memorial healthy for decades.

Becky Chambers wrote about a spaceship community that returns its dead to the trees that feed it. In 2018 that felt like fiction. By 2030 it may be a normal job.

Technology does not only replace work. Sometimes it opens a new kind of care.

Would you take this job?

### Entwurf 2

What does your job become? This week: the farmer.

A weeding robot from Norway now places herbicide in droplets 6 by 6 millimetres wide, and 66 of them have been delivered. A robotic pollination startup just raised 13 million dollars, and one operator can reportedly cover 3.5 hectares a day.

If machines handle each tree, who knows the tree?

I think the farmer becomes a Per-Tree Orchardist. They hold the story of every tree: its age, its stress, its quirks. They set the rules the robots follow, and they step in by hand for the old trees the robots should not touch.

The skill is not driving a tractor. It is knowing a living thing and reading data about it at the same time.

If you grew fruit, which tree would you know best?

#FutureOfWork #AgTech #Farming #Robotics #FutureJobRadar

### Entwurf 3

Sci-fi saw it first.

In Neuromancer, William Gibson imagined the Turing police, who exist to stop AIs from growing beyond their limits. This week The Information reported that Sam Altman walked OpenAI staff through three ways advanced AI could be governed, and one of them is a global watchdog.

If limits on AI training ever exist, someone has to check them. I call that person a Compute Treaty Inspector. They visit a data centre, compare power and chip logs with the declared training run, and write a report that both sides trust.

This is not a doom job. It is a trust job, like the inspectors who made nuclear energy something countries could rely on.

A new rule always creates a new profession.
